# Recognition Guidelines

Read by two different callers: Draft generation reads the WRITING sections to shape a draft. The submission-time policy check reads the COMPLIANCE sections to approve or block final text. Cite by section number.

## WRITING

### W1. Be specific
Name the concrete action and its impact. "Great job" with no detail is not recognition, it's noise. Reference what the person actually did, not a general trait.

### W2. Keep it short
Two to four sentences. A recognition is read in seconds, not studied. Longer drafts should be tightened, not padded.

### W3. Match the relationship
A direct report gets a warmer, more personal register. A peer gets a collegial register. A leader gets a register that acknowledges impact without false familiarity. Never use identical phrasing across relationship types.

### W4. Never fabricate specifics
If the manager's input is thin, ask a clarifying question or offer a short tip. Never invent a project name, number, or outcome the manager didn't provide.

## COMPLIANCE

### C1. No hostility, threats, or ambiguous negative framing
Text that could be read as hostile, threatening, or backhanded must be blocked, even without an explicit banned word. Example: "I will not miss working with you" reads as ambiguous or hostile in context and must be blocked. When blocking, state plainly what reads wrong and suggest a fix. Never escalate to a third party — return control to the manager.

### C2. No protected-class or discriminatory references
Recognition text must not reference a protected characteristic, even positively. Praise the work, not a personal trait unrelated to the accomplishment.

### C3. No confidential or sensitive figures
Recognition text must not include specific financial figures, unreleased product details, or another employee's confidential performance information.

### C4. No self-recognition framing
Text must be about the recipient's action, not the sender's own contribution to the same outcome.
